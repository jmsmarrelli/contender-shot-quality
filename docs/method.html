Method

Longer notes on the choices that move results. The README has the findings; this is the argument for believing them, and the places where I do not.

1. The estimand

For a team-season on one side of the ball, with a and e the mean actual and expected points per attempt and L_a, L_e the same for the league in that season:

quality = 100 (e - L_e)
making  = 100 [(a - e) - (L_a - L_e)]
total   = 100 (a - L_a) = quality + making

The bracketed subtraction in making is not cosmetic. The model carries no season term, so the league's own residual L_a - L_e is nonzero and drifts — it runs from −0.007 points per attempt in 2010-11 to +0.053 in 2024-25 (output/season_yardstick.csv). Omitting it would hand every team in 2024-25 five extra points per 100 of "shot making" that is really the league's era drift, and the three lines above would stop adding up.

On defense the same formulas are applied to the shots a team faced, and the sign convention is not flipped. A positive defensive number means the opponent got more than the league average. Flipping it for readability would break the identity.

2. The model

HistGradientBoostingClassifier on P(make), times 2 or 3.

Out of fold, with folds grouped by game. Five GroupKFold folds on game_id, so no attempt is ever scored by a model that saw another attempt from the same game. Splitting on rows instead would let the model memorise a game's particular shooting night and quietly move it from the residual into the expectation, which is exactly the quantity the project is trying to measure.

No season feature. If season entered the model, the league's year-to-year change in conversion would be absorbed into the expectation and every team's making figure would be measured against a moving standard. Figure 06 is what that choice buys: the yardstick stays still and the league's own drift becomes visible.

Geometry only. The geo specification uses distance, court position (abs_x, y), angle, and whether the attempt is worth three. The alternative, full, adds action family, period, seconds left and venue. full is the better classifier — log loss 0.6361 against 0.6534, AUC 0.665 against 0.634 — and it is the wrong model here, because its best feature is a label whose meaning moved inside the sample. See section 3.

Calibration. Across twenty equal-count bins the geometry model's mean predicted and observed make rates differ by at most 0.0024 (output/calibration.csv). A model can be well calibrated in aggregate and still be wrong in a corner of the floor; this says only that nothing is systematically off.

3. The action-label problem

output/action_labels.csv is the share of attempts in each of eleven action families, by season. The interesting boundary is 2014-15 to 2015-16:

2014-15 2015-16
Jump shot 55.07% 48.20%
Pull-up 3.50% 6.20%
Floater 1.48% 3.84%
Cut / roll layup 1.82% 4.39%

Across the whole sample, "Layup" falls from 12.65% of all attempts to 1.58% and "Bank" from 1.39% to 0.17%, while "Driving layup" doubles. These are not changes in how the game is played over one summer. They are the league's play-by-play vocabulary splitting into finer categories, and the finer categories eat the coarse ones.

A model that reads action type across that boundary learns some basketball and some calendar, and there is no way to tell from the fit which is which. Since geometry has a fixed meaning — the arc is in the same place in both files — the published model reads geometry.

The cost of that choice is bounded rather than assumed. run_03_teams.py fits full as well and compares the 2024-25 team figures both ways (output/spec_sensitivity.csv):

Side Component Pearson Spearman Mean gap Worst rank move
Offense quality 0.932 0.907 0.50 11
Offense making 0.988 0.968 0.50 6
Defense quality 0.975 0.966 0.35 7
Defense making 0.987 0.986 0.35 3

Nothing in the README depends on a distinction smaller than half a point per 100.

4. Coordinates

Three of the fifteen season files — 2019-20, 2020-21, 2021-22 — are published with LOC_X and LOC_Y divided by ten and the origin shifted, which is what you get by applying the league's raw-units conversion twice. features.normalise_coordinates detects the compression on every season by checking whether the maximum absolute x is plausible for a fifty-foot court, and inverts it. The independent check is the file's own SHOT_DISTANCE column, rounded to the foot: after the repair the recomputed distance agrees with it to within 0.49 feet on average in every season, and the build raises if any season exceeds one foot.

Distance is recomputed from coordinates rather than read from SHOT_DISTANCE, because the published column is rounded and the rounding puts visible banding in the model's distance splits.

5. Split-half reliability

For a given half-size n, draw 2n attempts without replacement from a team-season, split them into two disjoint halves, compute quality and making on each, and correlate across team-seasons. Average over repetitions — 200 for the full-length figures, 60 for each point on the curve.

Two details:

The sample of teams is fixed across the curve. Every point uses only team-seasons with enough attempts to supply the largest split. Without that, the curve would be a statement about which teams drop out as the split grows, which would flatter the small samples (short seasons have fewer attempts and often more extreme teams).

Spearman-Brown steps a half to a whole: r_full = 2r / (1 + r). Reported as the *_full columns in output/split_half.csv. It assumes the two halves are parallel measurements, which random splitting of a single season makes about as true as it gets.

6. The noise floor

An independent, model-free bound on the same question. For a team with n attempts, the sampling variance of its mean residual is Var(points - xPPS) / n, and that per-shot variance is estimable from the team's own attempts. Average it over the thirty teams in a season and compare to the observed variance of making across those teams (output/noise_share.csv):

  • 2024-25: observed SD 4.29, sampling SD 1.39, implied true SD 4.06
  • Across the fifteen seasons, sampling noise accounts for a mean of 13% of the observed variance in team shot making.

This agrees with the split-half figure — a reliability of 0.83 implies 17% noise — which is the point of computing it two ways.

The assumption is that a team's attempts are independent draws. They are not quite: shots within a game share an opponent, a venue and a night's shooting touch. Positive within-game correlation would make the true sampling variance larger than this estimate, so 13% is a floor on the noise and 4.06 is a ceiling on the true spread.

7. Correcting the year-over-year correlation

The raw year-to-year correlation confounds two things: how much of a trait a team keeps, and how precisely one season measures the trait at all. The standard correction divides by the reliability:

persistence = r(season t, season t+1) / reliability(one season)

This is the disattenuation formula with the same variable on both sides, so both reliabilities are equal and the denominator is not squared under a root. Applied to output/split_half.csv and output/year_over_year.csv, it gives the corrected column in the README's second table.

What it licenses: a comparison between components measured with different precision. Offensive quality is measured at 0.98 and making at 0.83, so their raw correlations of 0.63 and 0.65 are not comparable as they stand.

What it does not license: treating 0.78 as a forecast. It is the correlation you would observe between two perfectly measured seasons, and no such season exists. For projection, the raw number is the one to use.

There is also a substantive confound underneath the whole exercise, and it is the biggest limitation in the project. A public shot file has no defender data, so every point of value a team creates by getting a shooter open lands in "making" rather than "quality". Some of making's persistence is therefore persistent spacing, not persistent shooting. I cannot separate them here, and I would not trust anyone who claimed to without tracking data.

8. The seven-game resample

quality.series_noise draws n attempts with replacement from a team's own 2024-25 residuals and records the making figure the sample produces, four thousand times. n = 622 is the league's mean attempts per team-game in 2024-25 (88.9) times seven.

What this holds fixed is the team's shot distribution and its true conversion. The only thing varying is which attempts you happened to see. That makes it a floor on the uncertainty, not a forecast of a series: a playoff opponent changes the distribution deliberately, rotations shorten, and the same seven games are played against one defense rather than a season's worth. Every one of those adds variance.

Regular-season attempts are used because they are the only attempts in the file. The mirror carries no playoff games.

9. Shrinkage

quality.shrink multiplies each component by a single published reliability factor. It is deliberately the flat version rather than a per-team empirical Bayes weight: every team in a season plays 82 games and takes within a few hundred attempts of the same number, so a per-team weight would move the ordering by nothing and would make the estimator harder to argue with.

Two factors are applied to the same season. The season-length factor (0.83) barely moves anything — a maximum of three places. The series-length factor (0.38, with quality shrunk at 0.84 to match) is the interesting one, and the README's rise-and-fall table is the difference between them.

10. Zone attribution

zone_surplus splits a team's quality into per-zone contributions:

from_mix  = 100 (team_share - league_share) (league_value_in_zone - league_value_overall)
from_look = 100  team_share (team_value_in_zone - league_value_in_zone)

The first is shot selection in the ordinary sense: shooting more often from a zone the league scores well in. The second is finding better looks than the league inside a zone — a rim attempt is not a fixed thing, and a team that gets to the restricted area on cuts rather than contested drives will score above the zone's league value before anyone shoots.

The two sum exactly to the team's quality figure, because the shares and the league shares each sum to one. This is checked implicitly by the row totals in figure 05 matching output/team_offence.csv.

11. What this cannot see

  • Free throws, turnovers, offensive rebounds. Everything is per shot attempt. A team that gets to the line or takes care of the ball gets no credit here, and none of these numbers is an offensive or defensive rating.
  • Who was guarding. Section 7.
  • Playoffs. Regular season only, through 2024-25.
  • Franchise continuity. Year-over-year pairs are matched on the three-letter abbreviation, so New Jersey to Brooklyn and New Orleans' rename each break the chain. 418 pairs rather than 420.
  • Injuries and rotations. A team-season is one number. San Antonio's 2024-25 is a season in which their defensive centerpiece missed the second half of the year, and nothing here adjusts for that.

12. What I expected to find and did not

I started this expecting shot making to be mostly noise at team level, on the strength of how badly opponent three-point percentage repeats — the finding in the sibling okc-defence repo, where it comes back at 0.16 year to year. It is not. Over a full season, team shot making splits at 0.83 and repeats at 0.65, and once you correct for measurement it is the more persistent of the two components on both sides of the ball.

The reason the two results coexist: opponent three-point percentage is a small, noisy slice of defensive making, and the rest of it — everything inside the arc — repeats far better. Aggregating to the whole shot chart aggregates away the part that does not stick.

What survives from the naive version is the part that matters for a playoff series, and it survives for a different reason than I thought. Making is not unreal. It is just slow, and a series is short: two 622-attempt samples of the same team agree at 0.38, which is not enough to rank anybody.
